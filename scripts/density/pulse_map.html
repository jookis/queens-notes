Pulsing queen heatmap

The ordinary density map of the board, with the solutions blinking over it one after another. Each solution shows as its squares in a dark tone, and each one crossfades smoothly into the next: squares both share stay lit, and only the queens that change fade from one square to another. In build-up mode the heatmap starts empty and fills in from the solutions shown so far.

Similar ones together

Solutions are grouped by their patch on the diagonal sphere, largest patch first, and within a patch each next solution is the one with the fewest queens changed. That is still usually many: at 12×12 most steps change more than 6 queens, because few solutions have a close neighbour inside their patch. When exactly 4 queens change, it is almost always two separate swaps, not a rotation of four.

What the heatmap is

How often each square holds a queen across all solutions, relative to the average square. The squares near the corners are the emptiest, but no square is ever empty. In build-up mode the map starts from nothing and uses only the solutions shown so far.
