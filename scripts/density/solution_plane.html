Queen puzzle notes · density maps

One dot per solution family

Every solution, together with its turns and mirrors, is one dot. Two dots sit close when few queens differ between the boards after the best turn. Next to it are the same number of random rook placements (one per row and column, diagonals ignored), laid out the same way. Hover a dot to see its board.

What the plane shows

No separate groups. The dots form one blob for every size, and the two plotted directions hold only a few percent of the spread at n = 12. The linear families at n = 11 sit inside the blob, not apart from it.

What the plane hides

Up close, queen families come in chains. Many have a neighbour that differs by swapping just two queens, and linking families that differ in at most four queens joins almost all of them into one web. Random rooks at the same density stay isolated. The plane squeezes this web flat, so the links look like a tangle.

How the dots are placed

Classical multidimensional scaling of the distance "queens that differ after the best of the 8 turns and mirrors". It keeps large distances and does not invent clusters, unlike t-SNE or UMAP.
