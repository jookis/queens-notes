A heatmap on a sphere

Each solution of the flat board gets its own spot on a sphere, placed by two of its waves: how loud each wave is sets the height, and their phase difference sets the longitude. The colour shows how many solutions sit in each patch. Nothing is stacked on board squares. Drag to turn the sphere, and hover to see the boards at a spot.

Diagonal waves pile solutions up, in rows

With the diagonal and anti-diagonal waves, queen solutions share far fewer spots than random rook placements: 2,464 spots for 14,200 solutions at 12×12, with up to 168 on one spot. Their diagonal waves are also quieter. The diagonal rule makes solutions alike in exactly these two waves. The spots sit on few height levels (311 against 7,863 for rooks), which the unrolled map shows as rows.

Other waves spread them out

Searching all pairs of waves finds pairs that give most solutions their own spot (12,603 of 14,200 at 12×12, close to random rooks). These waves have no simple meaning, so the heat is harder to read.

Two mirror halves and a dense side

Flipping a board top to bottom swaps the two diagonal waves, so the lower half of the sphere is an exact mirror image of the upper half. A ring of spots just above the equator and its mirror below meet in a figure 8. Seen from the centre of the board, queen solutions lean towards the two waves being in step (longitude 0); random rooks do not.

Solutions with no spot

At 11×11, 88 solutions have both diagonal waves silent and get no spot. They are exactly the doughnut solutions, which have one queen on every wrapped diagonal.
