A heatmap on a sphere

Each solution of the flat board gets its own spot on a sphere, placed by two of its waves: how loud each wave is sets the height, and their phase difference sets the longitude. The colour shows how many solutions sit in each patch. Pick a patch and its solutions pulse through one by one, on the board and on the sphere at the same beat.

Diagonal waves pile solutions up, in rows

With the diagonal and anti-diagonal waves, queen solutions share far fewer spots than random rook placements: 2,464 spots for 14,200 solutions at 12×12, with up to 168 on one spot. The spots sit on few height levels (311 against 7,863 for rooks), which the unrolled map shows as rows.

Two mirror halves, a dense side, a repeat

Flipping a board top to bottom swaps the two diagonal waves, so the lower half of the sphere is an exact mirror image of the upper half. Seen from the centre of the board, queen solutions lean towards the two waves being in step (longitude 0). Around the sphere the pattern repeats 6 times at 12×12 and 5 times at 10×10.

What a patch has in common

At 12×12, two solutions from the same patch are linked by a small move (2 to 4 queens) about 10 times as often as two solutions picked at random. They share a few more queens than random pairs (1.17 against 1.04), but no square is used by all of them. So a patch is a loose neighbourhood of the web of small moves, not a fixed core. The pulse takes the closest remaining solution each time, but most steps still change many queens.
