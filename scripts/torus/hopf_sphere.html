Doughnut queens on a sphere

Every solution of the 13×13 doughnut board, together with all its shifts, is one kind. The Hopf map puts two waves of a board on a sphere and forgets their shared phase. With a wave and its double, every shift of a board lands on the same point, so each kind becomes one dot. Drag the sphere to turn it.

Why a wave and its double

A shift turns the phase of the wave at frequency w by some angle θ, and the wave at 2w by 2θ. Squaring the first also turns it by 2θ. Both now turn together, and the Hopf map forgets exactly that shared turn. With two unrelated waves the angles differ, and each kind spreads over 13 points.

What stays apart

Two waves together already give every kind shown its own point. Boards related by a turn, mirror or scaling usually land apart too, since those change the frequencies. The waves (1, 5) and (2, 3) are the exception: a quarter turn with a scaling leaves them unchanged, so there some related kinds share a dot.

Why most clean kinds are missing

A clean board sounds on only one line of frequencies, so each wave shows at most one clean kind. All 26 kinds that are not clean appear for every wave choice or nearly so.
